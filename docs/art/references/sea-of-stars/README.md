# Sea of Stars: references picked by Daniel (2026-11-11)

Twelve screenshots in this folder (`ref-01.jpg` to `ref-12.jpg`). Daniel likes the style a lot: this is the target
for every sprite, tile and effect. What they show:

- **ref-01, 03, 04**: the world seen from a slight top-down angle. Ground is painted, not flat: rock with big rounded
  stacked blocks, grass tufts, ferns, pines with fat layered foliage, sand paths, water with a white foam edge. Many
  props at many sizes; nothing is an empty flat colour.
- **ref-05, 07 (dark scenes)**: caves and night are dark purple/blue, but **the characters stay bright and
  saturated**, with warm and cold coloured light pools (orange torch, cyan magic) lighting the ground around them.
  Dark is a mood, never a mud: every sprite keeps its own readable colours.
- **Characters** (ref-01, 03, 04, 07): about 2.5 heads tall, big head, chunky readable silhouette, small feet, a
  hint of a dark outline that is tinted (not pure black), 2 to 3 shades per material, a bright rim on the lit side,
  tiny cast shadow. Hair, scarves and clothes carry strong colour (cyan hair, red tunic, yellow scarf).
- **Enemies** (ref-03, 04, 07): the same family as the heroes. Chunky, rounded, a little comic: a crab-like
  anglerfish with a glowing lure, a bug with pink armour and spikes, a stone golem covered in moss. Strong colour
  (pink, orange, teal) with a single glowing accent. Never grim, never realistic. Bosses (ref-05) are huge, take
  a third of the screen, with a clear weak spot that glows.
- **Portraits** (ref-08 to 12): hi-bit painted pixel art, thick shapes, warm skin, strong colour blocking, a
  readable face with personality, a flat colour card behind.
- **UI** (ref-03, 05, 07): small dark panels with thin gold/orange bars, tiny icons in rounded squares, clean pixel
  type. Info sits in the corners and never covers the action.
- **Effects** (ref-03, 04, 07): a crescent of cyan light with a glittering trail of small sparks; lights are the
  effects.

Rules that follow for Eterra Survivors:

1. Enemies must look like they belong to the same game as Aldo: same proportion (big head, chunky body), same
   outline treatment, same saturation. A Crypt enemy is dark in setting, not in colour treatment: give each one a
   strong colour and one glowing accent.
2. Size: enemies fill their hitbox with room to spare; an elite is about 1.5x a common enemy, a boss several times.
3. Ground: painted blocks, tufts, props at several scales, light pools; no flat tile colour.
4. Warm light against cold darkness is the way a dark level stays readable.
